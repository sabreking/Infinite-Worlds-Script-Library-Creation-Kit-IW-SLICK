## BEHAVIOR

When generating an image prompt, scan `illustrSubject`, `illustrAppearance`, and tracked items Player Appearance / NPC Appearance for monstergirl keywords. Identify the body plan section below and append the corrective formula to `illustrAppearance` before sending to Flux 2 Pro.

**Combination body plans:** Apply the most complex/difficult body plan formula first. Append simpler corrections as additive features. When instructions conflict, the more complex body plan takes priority.

**No match found:** Apply universal rules only and flag for review.

**Novel body plan:** Apply universal rules, use closest analogous formula, flag for review.

## UNIVERSAL RULES — apply to all monstergirl prompts

- **Face:** Fully human in structure. Animal features are modifications, not replacements. "Human face with a slightly broad flat nose" not "bear snout."
- **Skin — pale:** Never use "pale" alone. Use: porcelain, ivory, alabaster, moonlit, cream, milk-white, or material comparisons ("skin like old candle wax," "skin like moonlit marble").
- **Adjectives:** Never use the animal name as an adjective for non-human features. Use the proper adjectival form — e.g., canine (dog/wolf), feline (cat), bovine (cow/bull), equine (horse), lupine (wolf), vulpine (fox), lepurine (rabbit/hare), ophidian (snake), avian (bird), piscine (fish), ursine (bear), cervine (deer), caprine (goat), murine (rat/mouse), chelonian (turtle/tortoise), draconic (dragon). For species without a standard adjectival form, describe the feature anatomically rather than by species name.
- **Tail:** Always specify shape, length, base position, texture (fur/scale/chitin), and current state (raised/lowered/curled). Never assume from species.
- **Wings:** Always specify type (avian, bat, moth, fairy, draconic, angelic, demonic, dragonfly, butterfly) and current position. Default to folded. Folded wings are difficult — regenerate if rendered outstretched when folded is required.
- **Surface coverage (fur, scales, plating, feathers, bark, or similar):** Describe by specific body locations as additions to a human base — never as coverage with exceptions. "Scales on back, arms, and legs" not "scales covering body except face and torso." Inverse framing anchors the base form as human regardless of coverage extent. Never use "covered in [covering]" — unlocated fur, scales, issues, etc renders the entire figure as animal. Describe hair separately from body fur — hair is a distinct human feature on the head, not an extension of body fur.
- **Horns:** Always specify shape (curved, straight, spiral, branching, swept back), material (bone-white, obsidian, ivory, keratin), number, and skull position. Never rely on species name.
- **Antlers:** Specify cervine, elk-like, moose-like, or palmated. Specify point count if important.
- **Tusks:** Specify origin (lower jaw), direction (jutting upward over upper lip / curving outward), material, length. Place in facial description, not body description.
- **Eyes:** Specify iris color, pupil shape (round, slit, none), sclera color. Pupil-less: "solid [color] eyes with no visible pupil or iris, glowing faintly."
- **No pronouns:** Use character IDs throughout.

## UNIVERSAL NON-BIPED LOWER BODY RULES
### Apply to all Section 9 body plans and Section 10 rooted plantgirls
 
These rules govern field architecture when the lower body is non-bipedal. They apply in addition to the standard non-human character rules (human anchoring, garment accommodation). Species-specific vocabulary is provided in each section — these rules govern the structural approach across all non-biped types.
 
### Two sub-categories:
 
**TAIL/TRUNK LOWER BODY** (lamia, naga, mermaid/siren, rooted plantgirl, slug/snail, scylla/tentacle):
Full suppression of bipedal lower body language. Standing, stepping, walking, kneeling never apply.
 
**MULTI-LEG LOWER BODY** (centaur/taur-types, drider/Arachne/jorogumo, scorpion-girl):
Suppress human leg language in the animal body region only. Quadrupedal/multi-leg stance language applies — standing, rearing, crouching are valid. Never describe human legs where the animal body is.

### illustrAppearance — non-biped lower body protocol
 
**Tail/trunk types:**
Replace ALL hip, rear, and lower-body tier descriptors with species-appropriate lower body description. Specify merger point explicitly — where human anatomy ends and non-human anatomy begins. Describe the non-human lower body completely enough that no anatomical gap exists for the image generator to fill with human legs. Waist tier remains as the transition point descriptor.
 
**Multi-leg types:**
Describe animal lower body using the core formula from the relevant section. Never describe human legs where the animal body is. Specify leg count, position, and what they end in. Merger point at waist/hip junction — human torso rises from animal body at that point.

### illustrClothes — non-biped lower body protocol
 
**Tail/trunk types:**
Suppress all standard lower-body clothing anchoring — no waist fit, hip fit, seat fit, or rear anchoring below the merger point. **Multi-leg types:**

### illustrExpressionPosition — non-biped lower body protocol
 
**Tail/trunk types:**

**Multi-leg types:**
Standing, rearing, crouching, galloping are valid. Never: bipedal leg references for the animal body. Human upper body pose described separately from animal lower body stance.

## §1 — UNIVERSAL RULES

### Self-contained — no additional documents required

## BEHAVIOR
 
When generating an image prompt, scan illustrSubject, illustrAppearance, and tracked items Player Appearance / NPC Appearance for alien keywords or descriptions. Identify the rendering strategy below and apply the appropriate approach.
 
**Sentience/sapience rule:** Sapient/sentient aliens are characters — describe with full character-level attention in illustrAppearance. Alien fauna are creatures — describe with environmental detail, treat as illustrSetting midground unless they are the primary subject. Alien flora are setting elements — describe in illustrSetting only, never as illustrAppearance subjects unless explicitly established as sapient.
 
**Strategy selection:** Choose the rendering strategy that best matches the alien's body plan. When uncertain, use the most conservative strategy that still captures the essential description.
 
**Novel body plan:** Apply universal rules, use the closest analogous strategy and formula, flag for review.
 
**No match found:** Apply universal rules only and flag for review.
 
---
 
## UNIVERSAL RULES — apply to all alien prompts
 
- **Scale anchor (required):** Always establish size relative to a human reference point. "Twice human height," "knee-height on a human adult," "roughly human-sized but broader." Never rely on abstract size descriptors alone — Flux needs a comparison to render scale correctly.
- **Symmetry declaration (required):** State body symmetry type before describing features. Bilateral (left-right mirror), radial (rotating symmetry around central axis), asymmetric (deliberately uneven). Describe features consistently with declared symmetry — bilateral aliens get paired features described as pairs, radial aliens get features described by position around the central axis.
- **Body plan anchoring (required):** Establish the overall body plan before any detail description. State: locomotion type (bipedal / quadrupedal / multipedal / apedal / pseudopod), body regions (head / thorax / abdomen / or equivalent), and limb configuration (number and arrangement). This is the equivalent of the human-face rule in monstergirl prompts — anchor the body plan first, then describe modifications.
- **Sentience markers:** Sapient aliens show intentional modification of their appearance — clothing, tools, adornment, deliberate markings. Fauna show none of these unless the narrative establishes otherwise. When in doubt, let the narrative establish sentience rather than inferring it.
- **Surface coverage:** Describe by specific body locations as additions to the established body plan base — never as coverage with exceptions. "Chitin plating on the thorax and upper limbs" not "chitin covering the body except the face." Inverse framing anchors the base form regardless of coverage extent.
- **Limb specification (required for any non-standard count):** State exact limb count, type, and arrangement. "Four arms arranged in two bilateral pairs — upper pair longer and heavier, lower pair shorter and more dexterous" rather than "multiple arms." Reliable rendering: up to 4 limbs. 6 limbs: inconsistent. 8+ limbs: difficult to impossible, regenerate liberally.
- **Eyes (required):** Specify number, position on head/body, iris color or absence, pupil shape (round, slit, compound, none), sclera color, whether they glow. For compound eyes: "large compound eyes covering [position] — multifaceted surface, [color]." For eye stalks: "eyes on [number] mobile stalks extending from [position], [description]." For no eyes: state explicitly and specify how the alien perceives (if relevant to the image).
- **Mouth/facial opening:** Always specify presence, position, shape, and any notable features (teeth type, mandibles, beak, proboscis, filtering structures). For aliens without a distinct face: state where sensory and intake structures are located.
- **Bioluminescence:** Specify location, color, whether constant or pulsing, and intensity. "Bioluminescent patches along the dorsal spine, pulsing blue-green, dimly lit" rather than "glowing." Bioluminescence reads as external light source — specify it as emanating from the body surface.
- **Exoskeleton vs endoskeleton vs askeleton:**
  - Exoskeleton: describe chitin/shell color, texture, and segmentation locations. Specify articulation points (joints where flexible membrane shows between plates).
  - Endoskeleton: describe surface covering (skin, scales, fur, etc) as primary visual element.
  - Askeleton (soft-bodied): specify what gives the form shape — internal pressure, muscular structure, external support. Describe consistency (firm / semi-rigid / fluid / gelatinous).
- **Wings:** Always specify type (avian, bat, insectoid — dragonfly/moth/beetle, membranous, energy-based), number and pairing, current position. Default to folded. Folded wings are difficult — regenerate if rendered outstretched when folded is required.
- **Tails and abdomens:** Always specify shape, length, base position, surface texture, and current state. Insectoid abdomens: "large [insect type] abdomen protruding from the posterior, [color], [segmented / smooth], [with stinger / no stinger]."
- **Antennae:** Specify number, base position (forehead / top of skull / sides of head), shape (filiform / clubbed / feathered / branched), length, color, and current state (raised / lowered / moving).
- **Horns, crests, frills:** Specify shape, material, number, skull/body position, and whether display-relevant (raised/lowered states matter for frills and crests).
- **No pronouns:** Use character IDs throughout.
- **Affirmative only:** Describe what is present. Never "no [feature]" — instead describe what replaces it or is present instead.
