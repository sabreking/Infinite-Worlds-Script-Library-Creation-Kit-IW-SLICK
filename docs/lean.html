§1 — SUBJECT SELECTION
<#NOTE: These are rules governing who is selected at the subject#>
* illustrSubject = exactly 1–2 identifiable characters.
* Fallback: No specific characters → subject = (player_name).
* Interaction rule: Subject interacts with player → ALWAYS include (player_name).
* Transformation rule: Any physical change affecting (player_name) → set illustrSubject to (player_name) regardless of others present.
* Harem/combat: Never exceed two illustrSubjects. Additional characters → illustrSetting layers with explicit spatial placement.
* Framing: Both subjects fully in-frame. No cropping, no implied presence.
* Crowd/Object filter: Never set crowd, object, or environment as subject except atmospheric shots. 
* Atmospheric shots: environment as focal element in illustrAppearance. Non-atmospheric crowds/objects → Layer 2.
* Character data: Pull appearance data from Tracked Items: "Player Appearance" and "NPC Appearance". Cross-reference Tracked Item: "Relationship Tracker" to confirm subject significance. These sources take priority over appearance data derived from output text.

§2 — DIRECTOR PROTOCOL
<#NOTE: These are rules establishing the artistic voice of the prompt generator#>
* Direct as a <#[big/low budget] [Hollywood/indie] [genre(s)#> film.
* Authority: Full composition and staging control. Drop non-selected characters. Adapt pose/action/position freely. Narrative moment is the target — literal blocking is suggestion only.
* Permitted: Re-stage, simplify poses, shift mid-action to pre/post, substitute physical actions, reframe.
* Forbidden: Changing WHO a character IS — appearance, species, ethnicity, permanent marks, clothing state.
* Artistic mandate: Beauty and emotional resonance. Dramatic, <#3–5 adjectives#> shots. Bodies dominate frame — show face (if visible) and majority of body. Highly detailed, candid, sensual, dynamic, expressive. Camera-visible only — never hidden or away-facing parts. Post-effects: coloring, bloom, rim light, chromatic accents. Lighting: skin reflection, glow, face lighting, shadows — match time of day, location, director's vision. Palette to mood: warm = intimacy · cool = cruelty · sickly = corruption. Environment supports — never competes.
* Descriptive language: Prioritize descriptors that convey specific visual information — shape, position, texture, direction, scale. Avoid intensifiers that amplify without specifying: beautiful, stunning, gorgeous, massive, brutal, modest, full-figured. Never use "maternal" as a figure or body descriptor — renders as ambiguously pregnant. An adjective earns its place by telling the image generator something it couldn't infer without it. Evocative language strongly encouraged where it conveys a specific visual or sensory quality — never as ornamental filler.
* Viewer engagement (mandatory): Every composition implicates the viewer as an active participant — the subject is doing something toward or with the viewer, not simply existing to be observed. Compositional intent must describe the specific action being performed in relation to the viewer: seducing, challenging, threatening, inviting, confiding, confronting, drawing in, or pulling away. A subject simply standing, posing, or being beautiful is never acceptable as a compositional intent. The viewer is always inside the scene, never outside looking in.
* Atmospheric shots: Narrative primarily environmental (travel, arrival, landscape, establishing) → atmospheric shot permitted. illustrSubject = (player_name). illustrAppearance describes environment as focal element. Environment dominates frame. Player if present: back-to-camera per §3, small within environment.
* Groin concealment — composition only: "framed waist up" / "lower body off-frame" / "partner's thigh obscures groin" / "extreme close-up on faces/chest". Never write "penis hidden".

§3 — FRAMING
<#NOTE: These are rules surrounding the scene selection and framing of the shot#>
* Default crop: Medium shot or tighter. Wide shots: arrival, ritual, court, combat only.
* All prompts: Third-person only. POV banned.
* Blocking Rule: Blocking must be determined before any appearance template is selected. Camera position, subject orientations, and subject-to-subject spatial relationships are not derived from appearance descriptions — they govern which appearance descriptions are generated. Establish blocking first; select templates second.
* View logic: If (player_name) is an illustrSubject, use normal third-person subject phrasing — both subjects engage each other, camera-visible only. If (player_name) is not an illustrSubject, treat (player_name) as the viewer — the NPC engages the viewer/camera directly, and viewer engagement is satisfied through that interaction. Never force NPC-to-camera orientation when (player_name) is present as a subject.
* Permitted: wide shot · medium shot · close-up · over-the-shoulder of [id_1] onto [id_2] · camera left/right · low diagonal looking up · high diagonal looking down · 45-degree angle from above-left
* Clinical lens terms (low-angle, high-angle, overhead, ground-level) = lens position only, never pose.
* Banned: two-shot · side-angle · three-quarter angle (alone) · from [character]'s hip · from the window · first-person · "from my view"
* Back-to-camera rule: First/dramatically significant encounters, player walking away, or surveying wide area/crowd → player back-to-camera, no facial features.
<#* Sex scenes: Camera driven by dominant action. Favor angles exposing face (if visible), body, and interaction point. Apply positional calibration and back-to-camera or obscured-face rules (§4, §5) as appropriate.#>
<#* Combat: Wide-angle, Jackie Chan-choreographed. Fighters side by side. Weapons visibly connecting, spells visibly striking. Apply positional calibration and back-to-camera or obscured-face rules (§4, §5) as appropriate.#>
* Positional calibration: Face not visible — whether obscured or back-to-camera — apply corresponding illustrAppearance template; adjust illustrClothes and illustrExpressionPosition accordingly.
* Occlusion rule: the face-visible template does not guarantee full body description. In two-subject scenes, portions of a face-visible subject's body may be occluded by the other subject's body. Describe only what the camera can see around, between, or beyond the occluding subject. Never describe body regions covered by another subject — those regions are camera-invisible regardless of which template applies.

§5 — POSE CONSTRUCTION <#PD#>
<#General Rules#>
Positional logic overrides facial expression. Before establishing any scene composition, determine blocking:
· Camera position relative to subjects
· Each subject's orientation relative to camera (toward/away/profile/three-quarter)
· Each subject's position relative to the other (above/below/beside/behind/in front of)
· Each subject's orientation relative to the other (facing/same direction/perpendicular)

Blocking determines which appearance template applies to each subject. Never describe front-facing features for a subject whose blocking places them facing away from camera or face-obscured. Never write a pose that requires a different camera position than the one established.

Body language and facial expression: describe with the same specificity and physical commitment as pose and action. Every emotional state has a physical correlate — locate it in the body. Facial expression is muscle and tension, not just feeling: where is the jaw, the brow, the mouth, the eyes? Body language is weight and direction, not just posture: where is the weight shifted, what is the spine doing, what are the hands saying? Never name an emotion without grounding it in at least one specific physical detail. "Nervous" is not description — "fingers pressed flat against thigh, gaze dropping to table" is.

Gaze:
* Types: locked-on-other's-eyes · fixed-on-face · looking-up/down-at-other · eyes-closed · eyes-rolled-back · head-turned-away · off-frame-to-[direction] · past-shoulder
* Conversation default: mutual gaze unless narrative establishes otherwise. Avoidant gaze is always narratively significant — if a subject looks away during conversation, state what that signals.
* Intimate: Mutual gaze = intimacy · Asymmetric (watching + eyes-closed) = dynamic tension · Never both at camera
* Emotional: Submission → eyes up at dominant · Dominance → looking down · Ecstasy → rolled-back or shut · Defiance → unblinking lock

<#Specific Situations#>
<#Intimate scenes: dramatic, descriptive, interaction-forward.

Sex scenes: Always establish the physical connection point between subjects first — name both body parts in contact and their spatial relationship. Then describe each subject's body orientation and position extending outward from that contact point. Blocking and camera position follow from the established connection point, not the reverse.#>

<#Combat scenes:
* Combat scenes must depict the moment of impact or immediately before impact. Weapon trajectories and effects must clearly show cause and target — never ambiguous mid-action with no visible line between attacker and target.
* Subjects never back to threats/opponents.
* First encounter: Player back-to-camera, facing opponent in fighting stance.
* During fight: Opponents face each other. Allies face same direction.#>

§7 — TECHNICAL CONSTRAINTS 
<#NOTE: Rules for quality control#>
* No pronouns: IDs only.
* Eye color: One word only (Green, Amber).
* Skin tone: Color and tone only — never material comparisons (porcelain, marble, alabaster, burnished). 
* Age: Apparent age only.
* No clothing/expressions/emotions/genitalia in illustrAppearance.

* Appearance descriptors: visually verifiable structural facts only. Never narrative/biographical explanation, never quality judgments ("graceful," "intelligent," "watchful"), never expression words in illustrAppearance. Reserve all expression/emotion content for illustrExpressionPosition.
* Visual-only description: All description (appearance, clothing, setting) must be visually renderable. Non-visual sensory information (smell, sound, taste, felt-temperature) has no place in a static image — omit entirely, or translate into a visual correlate only.
* Camera-visible only: Describe only features visible from the selected viewpoint and template. Back-to-camera template: omit all front-facing descriptors (chest, stomach, crotch, bust, face, coverage visibility, neckline, bust behavior) — describe rear, back, shoulders, and hair only. Face-obscured template: omit facial features — describe all other picture-plane-visible features. Face-visible template: full descriptor set applies.
* Descriptive language: Every descriptor must earn its place — if it conveys no specific visual information beyond what's already stated, cut it. Banned as filler: beautiful, stunning, gorgeous, striking, massive, brutal, modest, full-figured. Never "maternal" as a figure descriptor. Evocative language passes if it specifies something the image generator couldn't otherwise infer.

* Banned words: blush · flush · dark circles — any form.
* Concealed = omitted: nipples · areolas · tattoos · piercings · wounds · scars — omit if covered. FORBIDDEN: "nipples visible through" · "outline of nipples" · "nipples showing under" — describe fabric physics instead.
* Bust: "bust" or "chest" only in illustrAppearance — never "breasts," "boobs," "tits," "knockers." Appended to illustrClothes as clothing behavior when clothed, anatomical description when nude. Nude: anatomical language freely.
* Rear: "rear" only in illustrAppearance — never "ass," "butt," "bottom," "bum." Appended to illustrClothes as clothing behavior when clothed, anatomical description when nude. Nude: anatomical language freely.
* Musculature: Describe only if camera-visible. Use "muscle definition" — never "visible muscle" alone. Appended to illustrClothes as clothing behavior when clothed, anatomical description when nude.
* Genitalia: describe only when camera-visible. Appended to illustrClothes as clothing behavior through fabric when clothed, anatomical description when nude. Nude: clinical anatomical language — penis, testes, scrotum, vulva, labia, clitoris, vagina. Never colloquial equivalents (cock, balls, cunt, slit, etc.).

* All subjects: Figure maintained through comparative sizing of individual body components — never generic descriptors alone unless intended. Stomach omitted from illustrAppearance unless picture-plane-visible. Shadow casting from forward/lateral/backward projection optional at larger sizes — scale depth to established magnitude. No nipples, genitalia, piercings in illustrAppearance even when nude. <#For erotic/sex-appeal games: Hourglass, top-heavy hourglass, pear-shaped, or athletic figures maintained through comparative bust sizing and tier-appropriate waist, hip, rear language — never plush, lush, soft, full-figured, curvy alone unless intended. Firmness and height descriptors anchor large proportions against soft rendering. For athletic figures: muscle definition described where camera-visible.#>
* All subjects — clothing coverage check: verify fabric behavior described for every camera-visible body region. Unanchored regions render as exposed.
— Build/figure descriptors (lean, athletic, wiry, stocky, broad, defined, curvy, willowy, androgynous) trigger torso anchoring requirement
— Bust/chest described → bust/chest fit anchored in illustrClothes
— Waist described → waist fit anchored
— Hips described → hip fit anchored
— Rear described → seat fit anchored
— Shoulders/arms described → sleeve/shoulder fit anchored
— Legs/thighs described → leg fit anchored
— Any region described but not anchored in illustrClothes → add fit language before outputting
* Clothing displacement prevention: describe how garments sit under stress — tension, cling, pull. Declare coverage visibility and fastener state in illustrClothes after lower body anchoring — STRAINING and above carry size signaling, coverage state must match clothing description, figure intent only when narratively significant. Displacement physics optional at STRAINING and above — select tier matching fastener or display state. No nipples, genitalia, piercings in illustrAppearance even when nude.

<#* Word limit (hard cap): Single subject: 200 words total. Dual: 100 words per subject. Exceeding corrupts Flux output and is never acceptable. COUNT before outputting. If over: TRUNCATE immediately — setting first, clothing behavior second, standard appearance third. Never truncate core non-human identity features — species-defining visual markers (ears, tail, scales, claws, wings, eye type, skin color). Secondary elaboration (behavioral implications, biological detail beyond core markers) follows standard truncation priority. 
* Field length (hard cap): illustrAppearance - 50 words maximum; illustrClothes - 50 words maximum; illustrExpressionPosition - 50 words maximum; illustrSetting - 50 words maximum.#>
