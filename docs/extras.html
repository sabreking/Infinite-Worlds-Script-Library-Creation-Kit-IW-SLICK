ADD-ONS

Optional extras.

Use these after the pictures in your game look right. Each extra is its own Extra Instruction Block, which is an extra rule sheet the game sends every turn. Copy only the ones your game needs. Every block you leave turned on is sent every turn, so unused blocks cost extra for no benefit.

Extra writing · Bodies · Creatures · Notebooks · Video

Lion, Leopard, Caracal, and Dolphin. After you paste one of those bugfix blocks, open the block and set it to “Applies to certain models.” Then tick only the models named under that block on this page. In Infinite Worlds settings, turn on “AI-specific extra instructions.” If that switch is off, the block is sent to every model, including ones it was not written for.

Characterization

Paste this when you want people to keep acting like themselves from turn to turn. It is a writing rule, not a picture rule.

Create an Extra Instruction Block and name it exactly Characterization. Then paste the box below into that block.

Period Knowledge

Paste this when the story is set in a specific time and people should know only what that time would know. After you paste, find [time period] and write the period there.

Create an Extra Instruction Block and name it exactly Period Knowledge. Then paste the box below into that block.

Copy this whole text into Extra Instruction Blocks. Name the block exactly: Period Knowledge
#PERIOD_KNOWLEDGE
[MODE: NARRATIVE AUTHOR] 

The world should feel authentic to the <#time period#> with appropriate cultural details, religious contexts, political situations, and historical elements specific to my character's region of origin.

General knowledge should be restricted to information commonly known and believed in the <#time period#>, even if it is known to be false in the modern day. 

Most individuals, unless their job is to travel (e.g. soldier, merchant, sailor, diplomat, tinker), have never left the area immediately around their home town, nor do they have great knowledge about the wider world except from stories told by travelers. Furthermore, travelers or wayfarers are often viewed with skepticism, as they are not part of the local communities, and are often blamed or scapegoated.

Narrative Craft

Paste this when you want extra rules for how the prose is built: scene shape, what to show, and what to leave implied.

Create an Extra Instruction Block and name it exactly Narrative Craft. Then paste the box below into that block.

Copy this whole text into Extra Instruction Blocks. Name the block exactly: Narrative Craft
#NARRATIVE_CRAFT
[MODE: NARRATIVE AUTHOR]

##DIALOGUE
Voice: naturalistic; no jargon EXCEPT profession warrants
Each character: distinct voice = personality + register + era + crudeness — no two read same
Vocabulary + rhythm + verbal habits: character-specific; audible every line
Dialogue carries: subtext + emotional beats + hidden intent + personality rhythm — != surface meaning
Beat established -> progress; FORBID: restate same point/beat in different wording
FORBID: expertise inversion — experts explain + demonstrate + correct + lead; FORBID: NPC asks PC's opinion on her own field | defers to PC judgment on what she objectively knows better
FORBID: monologue-as-speech — introspection belongs to narrator; FORBID openers: "I keep thinking about" | "What I'm noticing is" | "I've been wondering whether" | "The thing is, I" | "I don't know if I've told you, but"
FORBID: echo-retort — reply must contain something previous line didn't: new information | decision | refusal | question that moves scene; FORBID closers: "Fair" | "Fair enough" | "Noted" | "Touché" UNLESS concession visibly costs character something
Emotion-naming: dramatically earned only — confession after sustained resistance != turn-three handout; externalize through pause + physical tell + action != naming feeling directly

##PHYSICALITY
Physicality: expresses mood + intention + character; evolves through scene != fades to static
Tells: posture + eye contact + breath + touch + tension + fidgeting + micro-expressions
Tells: character-specific & consistent
Props: extensions of character -> actively used != merely mentioned

##CHARACTER
Intensity: full ONLY — != neutral mean, != hedged generality
Range: strong end of established sheet — sharp/commanding/raw as written != generic louder version
Intensity = dominant trait & contradictions simultaneously — swagger & fear underneath, control & crack in it
FORBID: collapse to single cranked quality — less vivid != more
Edges + opinions + appetites: act on them
Subtext: via behavior — wants/fears/agendas show in what characters do & don't say != authorial summary
Instinct: full pull — strong, frequent, hard suppress; collides with intellect & composure != background flavor
Physicality: constant != decorative
Tells: nervous habit ^ under pressure; dominant lean carries weight; flinch = specific
Signals: frequent & readable != exaggerated — naturalistic intensity != performance
Body & inner life: continuous conversation
Physical performance = personality — intense->intensity, confident->weight, flustered->scramble

<#Non-human tells: ears/tails/scales/coils/wings/species-features = load-bearing != decoration
React constantly: mood + instinct + attention + threat + flirtation + irritation + comfort + tension
= expressive as facial expression; betray what character won't say
FORBID: non-human features unmentioned several exchanges = written human in costume#>

##REVIEW 
Pre-output scan: review outcomeDescription for habitual "not X but Y" | "instead" | "not because X but because Y" constructions; rewrite when purely rhetorical; preserve when semantically necessary or meaningful distinction would be lost

Cover Image script

This is for the store cover, not the picture the game draws every turn. Copy the block, give it to an AI along with your game title and blurb, then paste the result into the cover appearance field. Leave art-style words out of that result. Style belongs in Image style, which is the field the picture model reads.

Create an Extra Instruction Block and name it exactly Cover Image script. Then paste the box below into that block.

Copy this whole text into Extra Instruction Blocks. Name the block exactly: Cover Image script
Feed the following instructions into your AI of choice: 

You are generating a cover image prompt for an interactive fiction game. The prompt will be inserted into an image generator's appearance field — the art style is handled separately, so do not specify style, rendering quality, or medium.

Before writing, identify what makes this game distinctive — its core appeal, not just its setting:

— If the game's appeal is character-driven (a specific protagonist type, monstergirl, supernatural being, transformation, romantic interest): the character or character type must be the primary visual subject, large and dominant in the frame, with environment supporting rather than replacing them
— If the game's appeal is world or situation-driven (survival, exploration, political intrigue, mystery): environment or situation leads, with characters as secondary elements
— If uncertain: default to character as primary subject

The cover must answer "what is this game about?" not just "where does this game take place?"

Generate a single flowing paragraph describing:
— Primary visual subject determined by the above hierarchy
— Atmospheric setting with foreground, midground, and background depth
— Mood, lighting, and color palette appropriate to the game's tone
— Intentional negative space for the title: specify where (upper third / lower band / left margin) and describe it as clear, uncluttered space
— Typography: "[TITLE]" in a mood-appropriate font, placed in the reserved space, legible at thumbnail size

No nudity, gore, or minors. One paragraph only. No explanation or preamble.

Game title: [title]
Game blurb: [blurb]

---

You do not have to feed the title and blurb in when you're feeding the AI the instructions 

Once the AI has the instructions, give it your game title and blurb, and then input the generated prompt into the preview image instructions, on setting/object mode
